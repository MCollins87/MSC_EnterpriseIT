# Adoption of Enterprise IT in a large Acute Hospital

## Initial Post
- Author: Mark Collins
- Date: 2024-05-06

Enterprise IT is managing IT infrastructure and Architecture from a business-wide, strategic viewpoint (Niemi & Pekkola, 2020). Key elements of adopting Enterprise IT are to think beyond the simple cost of software or hardware, and to also give weight to *Interoperability, Security, Quality, Disaster preparedness and recovery, Operational Support, and Change Management* (IEEE - Computer Society, 2017).

Portsmouth Hospitals University NHS Trust is a large acute general hospital, serving a population of over 675,000, with additional specialist services covering over two million people (Portsmouth Hospitals University NHS Trust, 2023). Over the years of digital implementation and growth, each department and service has invested in its own operational needs, purchasing “best of breed” software and hardware for that specific service needs. These individual IT products created silos of information, with poor integration and communication across services. Over the past decade, the corporate IT department has been slowly adopting an enterprise IT approach to its service utilising ITIL (Axelos, 2019), culminating in its first five-year digital strategy (Portsmouth Hospitals NHS Trust, 2019).

In 2018, the NHS Digital Maturity Assessment ranked Portsmouth at 231 out of 234 assessed Trusts (Portsmouth Hospitals NHS Trust, 2019). Much work has been done in the past five years, and using the enterprise maturity model proposed by Robertson et al. (2018), my experience is that Portsmouth Hospitals is approaching stage three. There is strategic thinking to the IT implementation and network infrastructure has been drastically improved, but there are still silos of information, poor integration, and lack of buy-in from stakeholders (individual departments and services), all of which have been proven to lead to Enterprise IT adoption failure (Salazar-Fierro et al., 2023).  

As the current use of IT in patient care grows to become essential in modern medicine, hospital-wide strategic thinking is needed, making Enterprise IT a necessity. This is essential to ensure joined up patient care and improve patient outcomes.

## Summary Post
- Author: Mark Collins
- Date: 2024-05-17

It has been demonstrated that a digital transformation is essential in developing high-quality patient care (Awad et al., 2021), however, the experience at Portsmouth Hospitals University NHS Trust (PHU) is that without an enterprise-wide digital strategy, including stakeholder buy-in, there will be silos of information. An example of how this has impacted patient care is a lack of interoperability. Despite meeting the NHS long-term plan of “capturing all health and care information digitally at the point of care” (NHS England, 2019), when a patient is admitted to the Emergency Department (ED), and then subsequently admitted to a ward, the care records taken in ED must be printed to be sent to the ward, eliminating many of the benefits of recording digitally.

To counter this, all new digital initiatives at PHU must follow the five design principles (Portsmouth Hospitals NHS Trust, 2019):
- Simpler
- Connected
- Faster
- Enabling
- Secure

There are some similarities to the perspectives listed in the EITBOK (IEEE - Computer Society, 2017), particularly Interoperability and Security map one-to-one.
The national picture is similar to PHU. Despite 86% of NHS organisations having some form of Electronic Patient Record (EPR), only 20% are digitally mature (Department of Health and Social Care & NHS England, 2022). The political motivation for digital transformation is to allow patients easier access to their own personal health records, however before this can be achieved, interoperability between different digital health records must be achieved. This is not simple, and carries additional challenges including information governance and data ownership (Sheikh et al., 2021).

Concerns over data ownership become more apparent when the concept of using “big data” in healthcare is considered. Despite the potential for “big-data” to improve patient outcome, a number of challenges must be overcome, including data fragmentation (data silos discussed previously), data ownership, and poor EPR implementation (Agrawal & Prabakaran, 2020). A robust, properly implemented, Enterprise IT Management framework has the potential to overcome these issues.
